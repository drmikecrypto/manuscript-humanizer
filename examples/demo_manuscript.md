1. Introduction and Background
Diabetes mellitus is one of the most common metabolic and endocrine disorders, characterized by impaired glucose metabolism and various anabolic and metabolic abnormalities. This disease is primarily divided into two main types: Type 1 diabetes, which is insulin-dependent, and Type 2 diabetes, which is non-insulin-dependent. Type 2 diabetes requires continuous care and regular treatments, as it may lead to serious complications such as hyperglycemia, ketosis, hyperosmolar shock, coma, cardiovascular diseases, arteriosclerosis, ocular conditions, renal and neurological diseases, severe infections, and even death.
2. Materials and Methods
In this study, male Wistar rats weighing 190-210 grams were randomly divided into 8 groups. Group 1 (control) received no treatment. Group 2 (diabetic control) received physiological serum for 2 weeks and then were induced with diabetes using streptozotocin, followed by an additional 6 weeks of physiological serum. Groups 3 to 6 received physiological serum for 2 weeks, were induced with diabetes, and then treated with nettle extract, fenugreek extract, and a combination of both extracts, respectively, for 6 weeks. Group 7 received physiological serum for 2 weeks, were induced with diabetes, and then treated with glibenclamide for 6 weeks.
3. Results
Measurements of blood glucose, insulin levels, and body weight were performed at various stages: before diabetes induction, 2-3 days after diabetes induction, 2 weeks after the start of treatment, and 6 weeks after diabetes induction. The results indicated that nettle and fenugreek extracts, both individually and in combination, were effective in reducing blood glucose levels, increasing insulin levels, and decreasing body weight in diabetic rats. Additionally, the efficacy of these extracts was comparable to the commonly used diabetes drug, glibenclamide.
4. Discussion and Conclusion
This study suggests that nettle and fenugreek extracts could be considered as complementary herbal medicines in the treatment of diabetes. The results indicate that these extracts can effectively reduce blood glucose levels and increase insulin levels while also reducing body weight. However, to confirm these findings and determine the appropriate dosage, clinical studies on humans are necessary. Such studies could help scientists and physicians develop the best therapeutic strategies for diabetes management and improve the quality of life for diabetic patients.
